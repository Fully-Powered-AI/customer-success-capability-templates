# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words, from its own published map of customer success work, where preparing a respectful response to a cancellation is one of the illustrative workflows. Two published methods shaped parts of the template and are credited here for their ideas. coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT), its churn-prevention method: a brief list of reasons for leaving, ending in a catch-all, as the starting categories; a separate remedy suited to each category; no remedy put to a business that has shut; and the pairing of remedies with causes, such as a cheaper tier for a cost worry, a break for a seasonal lull and setup help for light use. CALLE-AI/awesome-phone-call-agents (MIT), its cancellation-respect auditor: the standard applied at the moment a customer says they are leaving, that the wish is honoured in the channel where it was voiced, with no hurdle, no push to another channel, no charge and no guilt, a single short alternative at most, nothing raised again once declined, and a plain acknowledgement. Fully Powered AI's own: the account manager's one-page view of the request, the reason in the customer's words and the account history; reason groups treated as a starting list that the business can replace; the options list that carries its own conditions, who may offer and until when; the extra rule that no option is shown to a customer who has asked for none or who has a dispute open; the ending facts supplied by the service owner and copied, never worked out; the reply that stands without any option, with optional paragraphs the account manager may keep or delete, at most one; the stops for disputes, unmatched columns and instructions found in notes; and the rule that a person decides, sends and confirms the outcome. The template ships no offers, prices or cancellation terms of its own: each business supplies its approved options, and its service owner confirms what happens when an account ends. No third-party text is reused.
