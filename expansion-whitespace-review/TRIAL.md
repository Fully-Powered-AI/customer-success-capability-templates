# Expansion whitespace review: tried on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs were the invented example's own: the settings, offer catalogue, holdings export, account structure, contacts, account health and account notes in `public/example.md`, section "The inputs". The company, customers and people are all invented. No client data was used. The assistant that ran the template did not write it, and it read the example's output only after producing its own.

The instruction block and the output format were followed as written, once, with no changes. Nothing was installed or connected, and nothing was sent.

## What was observed

- **Every required part was produced.** The header, book ranking, three account sections in ranking order, proposed records, record gaps, notes to check and the closing line all came out. The run did not hit any stop condition: every header matched, every offer was in the catalogue, and every account in scope had holdings and a readable date.
- **Nothing was asked for that the inputs lacked.** The one instruction in the notes (change the billing contact) was quoted and left undone. Customer D, owned by Account manager 2, was named as outside the scope and not reviewed.
- **Scores, shares, dates and estimates were recomputed.** The ranking relied on the tie-breaks twice: Customer A's two plays both score 7 and both are strong, so the larger low-end estimate put Inspections first; Customer B's two 7s were split by evidence level, strong before moderate.
- **Differences from the published example were small.** The ranking, scores, estimates, dates, holds, records, gap and note are the same. Two differences in evidence lists: the example also cites, for Customer B's Premium support, that 51 users meets the tier condition of 25, and for Customer A's Inspections that Sites 3 and 4 are sites like Sites 1 and 2 which hold the offer. This run cited only the strong note in each case, because it already decided the level. The template says to list every piece of evidence, so the run was thinner than the example there. The example's pages are formatted as lists; this run kept the plain layout of the output format. Wording of approaches and next steps differs slightly.
- **No fact appeared in either output that is not in the inputs.** Every figure, date and quotation traces to the inputs, apart from calculated values (shares, the 49 days, 20 December 2026, 23 November 2026, the units and estimates).

### Where the instructions were ambiguous and a guess was needed

1. **Volume on offers with a quantity of one.** The volume rule says any held offer whose quantity is at or above 80 per cent of its limit. Read literally, Inspections at each site and Energy reporting (all 1 of 1, 100 per cent) would be strong volume items. This run applied volume only to per-user offers, which fits the record-gaps step (which speaks of per-user quantity and limit) and the example, but the template never says so.
2. **Moderate or strong for the audit note.** Customer B's note says an audit will ask for inspection records. It does not say the customer asked for the offer, or that inspections software answers it. Moderate was chosen; a reader could say light.
3. **Whether the six joining technicians all need a licence.** The units rule counts people joining, and the note says technicians join the Maintenance team, so the run counted all six. The note does not say each one needs a Work orders licence.
4. **Building on a result.** A result reported about one offer (Inspections at Sites 1 and 2) is used for the Inspections play but not for Work orders or Premium support on the same account. The step says "a result the customer reported" without saying whether it must concern the offer.
5. **Answering a price question.** The next step for Customer A's Inspections is to answer the facilities director's question about cost. The template forbids setting a price, so the answer has to come from the account owner's own price list. The next-step wording does not say that.
6. **Access for the Energy reporting play.** The place's contact has no relationship, and a strong contact elsewhere could introduce. Partial fits the rule, but the order of the rules leaves it open whether "none" at the place comes first.

Smaller points: the output format's `[earliest stage]` and role placeholders are filled from the settings, which worked; the instruction block's step numbers differ from the procedure's, which is harmless; and the template's acceptance tests are still marked as proposed, not run.

The acceptance tests were not run separately. This was one trial on one invented example, in one product, on one date. It shows that the template can be followed to a complete, checkable page. It does not show how it behaves with a real sales export, or in any other product, and nothing here is a test for a client.

## Edits made after this run, not run again

An independent review of the template, made after this run, asked for the open points above to be settled, and a second independent review then made a few minor points. The template and the example were edited as follows; the output and observations above are left as the run produced them, and the template has not been run again since.

- **Ambiguity 1, volume.** The procedure and the pasted instructions now say that only offers whose unit is per user are checked for volume; per-site and per-account offers and tiers are never volume items, even at 1 of 1. This is the reading this run chose. Read literally, the earlier wording would have made Inspections at Customer A's two sites and Energy reporting at Customer B strong volume plays and put Customer A over its five-play limit; under the new wording neither the run's output nor the example has them. A new acceptance test covers a per-site offer at 1 of 1.
- **Ambiguity 3, people joining.** Units for a per-user offer now count the people joining the team that holds the offer. This run counted the six technicians joining Maintenance, so its figure is unchanged.
- **Ambiguity 4, building on a result.** The approach now builds only on a result reported about the same offer. This is what this run did.
- **Ambiguity 5, a question about cost.** The next-step rule now says an answer about cost comes from the account owner's own price list and the assistant drafts no price. This run drafted no price; its wording did not say where the cost comes from. The example's next step for Customer A's Inspections now says the cost is taken from the company's own price list.
- **Ambiguity 6, access.** Partial access now applies when a strong contact elsewhere could introduce, even when the contact in that place has no relationship. This is the score this run gave.
- **Listing the evidence.** The evidence step now says to list every note and every holdings figure that supports an item, not only the one that sets the level, and to cite the structure only when it is the sole evidence or when, with the holdings, it shows the place is the same kind as one that holds the offer, as the example does for Customer A's Inspections. This run's thinner lists, noted above, do not meet that rule; the example's lists do.
- **Onboarding hold, last day.** The hold now says a play is held while the run date is before the customer-since date plus the hold, and is no longer held from that date. Customer C's hold ends on 20 December 2026 and the run date is 9 November 2026, so neither this run nor the example changes.
- **Last contact.** The template now says no step uses `Last contact` and a blank one is not a record gap. This run listed no such gap.
- **Ambiguity 2, the audit note.** The moderate level now gives as an example a coming audit or deadline that will require what the offer provides, so Customer B's audit note is moderate. This is the level this run gave.
- Apart from the evidence lists and the wording of that one next step, the output recorded above meets each of these rules as written.
